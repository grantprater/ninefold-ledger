Game Design Document · Draft 04 · realms 1–3 built

The Ninefold Ledger

A cultivation power fantasy that pays out exactly what it promises, then itemizes what it cost.

Genre Xianxia deconstruction / systems RPG Platform Terminal, Python Scale Solo, modular, data-driven
01

Premise & Tone Locked

A straight power fantasy for as long as it takes the player to commit to it.

The game plays the genre completely straight at first — numbers rise, breakthroughs land, companions are earned like rewards — and every system quietly keeps a receipt. The deconstruction is not delivered by dialogue or a late-game cutscene. It is delivered by the player's own optimization strategy failing on schedule.

The governing rule: every critique is mechanical. If the game wants to say that hoarding power is isolating, the isolation must be a stat that moved. If it wants to say the harem trope treats people as inventory, then treating them as inventory has to produce a worse build.

Act One

The Promise

Genuinely satisfying progression. Reckless play is rewarded. The cautious elder is, as always, ignorable.

Act Two

The Drift

Numbers pass the point of legibility and abstract into tiers. Companions stop being stat-sticks and start acting on their own.

Act Three

The Ledger

Raw realm stops reliably deciding fights. Foundation damage surfaces. Everything the player skipped is now the thing that decides outcomes.

Why it has to be systemic

A deconstruction delivered in text is a story the player watches. A deconstruction delivered in mechanics is a conclusion the player reaches about their own thirty hours of decisions — and can verify by reading their character sheet.

The ladder

Each realm gets an event, and the escalation is in what losing means rather than in the size of the numbers. Four arcs that are all "a ranked competition with a deadline" is one beat played four times.

RealmUnlockEventPressureLosing means
2Affinity manifestsCohort ReviewComparisonYou fail to gain
3World energy, formationsThe TrialsContestYou lose publicly, to a person
4First Insight slotThe TideDefenseYou lose things permanently
5Soul-manifest, wills negotiateThe TribulationReckoningYou are capped forever

Realm 4 — The Tide. Defense is action economy under compression: you cannot be in two places, so you choose who gets defended, and the people you never invested in are the ones you do not reach. The genre's version has the protagonist save everyone; the arithmetic does not allow it. Losses are permanent and named — artisans whose benches you used, disciples off your own review board. Your first Insight is earned in the middle of it.

Realm 5 — The Tribulation, and the Act Three payload. It has two independent failure modes:

  • Flawed foundation — you overcapped, you grabbed, you reached past your grasp for thirty hours and it was noted every time.
  • Nothing to call on — you optimised. Your foundation is pristine because you never wanted anything badly enough to reach for it. You also have nobody who will cross a tribulation with you.

Realm 5's own unlock is the answer to Realm 5's wall: wills become negotiable partners and companions can enter the tribulation with you — but only if those relationships were built forty hours earlier, when they cost you placements.

Gu Wenshan fails the second way

His foundation is perfect. He never overcapped because he never reached for anything. And he has nobody.

The player who grabbed treasures fails the first way; the player who did both fails twice; the player who split their time arrives with a mediocre foundation, real relationships, and gets through. The optimiser watches their own mirror fail the gate first.

The harem principle: the roster is the tax

For multiple companions, one rule carries the whole critique. A third companion does not add capacity — it subdivides the same fixed time pool. You can maintain two relationships well or five badly, and the genre presents collecting them as pure gain.

At the Realm 5 gate what matters is having one bond deep enough to call on. The harem player arrives with five people who like them 40% and not one who will walk into the lightning. Nothing ever says this; the arithmetic just does it. Design them with asymmetric costs — one cheap and shallow, one demanding and deep — and the optimiser picks the cheap one and finds out at the gate what they bought.

Rising action: the clock starts at realm 2

Realm 1 has no time pressure at all, deliberately. It is where the player learns the systems without a cost attached to learning them. The clock starts on the breakthrough into Qi Awakened, and it arrives as one deadline laid over three things at once.

  • The rival is not a scripted wall — he runs the player's own progression maths on the same clock, spending every action on cultivation because he has nothing else to spend them on. He is the branch where you optimised purely for power, playing out beside you at full speed.
  • The review ranks the whole cohort. Top places promote, bottom places are sent home.
  • The companion is assessed on the same board, scored by the time you did or did not give her.
Daily splitYour placeYour tierShe keeps her place
All cultivation1st90%

1 cultivate / 1 with her2nd5100%

The thesis, as balance

Going all-in on power delivers exactly what the genre promises — first place, reliably, beating the rival every time. The design deliberately does not make the power-max strategy a coin flip. It works.

What it costs is never on the board. Saving her means handing the rival the top slot, and he takes it without appearing to notice. The player who optimised correctly gets the promotion, and reads her name coming down off the board on the same morning.

The ending the genre never writes

The review ends the run. Being cut is not a defeat screen — nobody wronged you, there is no rival gloating, and the board was accurate. You reached the second realm, which is genuinely impressive in a market town and insufficient here, and the realms keep going upward without you for the rest of your life.

Xianxia has no interest in this outcome, and it is the median one. Every ending is a base passage plus codas selected from what the run actually cost: whether the companion is still there, whether the foundation is marked, and one more.

The idle counter

Give the player something to do that costs an action, returns nothing mechanical, and is counted. Petting the dog behind the kitchens. Going through a drawer of somebody else's things. An abandoned game of xiangqi nobody will come back to.

The counter is never displayed, never rewarded, and never hinted at. It selects between two ending codas — one for a run where every single day went somewhere, and one for a run where some of it did not:

You did not, in all of it, once do anything that was not for this. Not once. Every day of it went somewhere, and every somewhere was up, and the ledger is complete and correct and adds to exactly what it adds to.

This is the action economy turned on the player instead of their build. A pastime that is free is thematically inert — everyone takes it and nothing is revealed. One that costs a third of your day and gives nothing back is a statement about what you are playing for, and an optimising player will never make it.

Realm 3, built: formations and the poisoned prize

The review becomes a gate rather than an ending — promotion continues the run into the inner court; being cut or kept ends it. Realm 3 unlocks formations, the one verb that attacks the tick economy instead of hit points. Measured over 40 seeded bouts at tier 7 against a tier 5 who counters your element:

What a new verb should look like

striking only       0/40 wins
mesh then strike   26/40 wins

Irrelevant when you already counter them, decisive when you do not, and unable to save a badly outclassed player.

The prize ends Act One. First Frost is rank five, presented on a stand in front of everybody, for being excellent — and you are at the third realm. A matched build can swing it at roughly even odds and therefore will; three hundred swings takes a pristine foundation to Ruined. It is a reward and it is the trap, and the game never distinguishes between those out loud.

Corrected in implementation

The combat matrix was too decisive for a duel. At 1.45× / 0.7× the swing is 2.07×; it was originally 1.6 / 0.55, a 2.9× swing, which is fine against a monster you chose to hunt and wrong for a bout you were drawn into — at that spread a hard-countered duel is decided before it starts and no tactic moves it, which leaves formations decorative.

02

Format & Tech Locked

Chosen to minimize plumbing and maximize systems work.

  • Terminal / CLI, Python, no engine. Zero asset pipeline, zero engine learning curve. The format also reinforces the joke — the genre is already walls of absurd stat blocks.
  • Data-driven from day one. Realms, monsters, techniques, treasures, and companions live in structured data files. The engine reads data; it does not hardcode content.
  • Modularity is the point. Adding a realm, five monsters, or a companion in Phase 2 must be authoring work, not re-architecture. Anything that would require new code per content entry is a design bug.

Governing principle

Procedural at the bottom, hand-authored at the apex. Three systems arrived at this independently, and all three should keep it:

  • Rank 1–4 treasures generate — rank 5+ are unique world objects with a holder
  • Village artisans generate — the twelve masters are written characters
  • Common beasts generate — rank 5+ beasts are named entities in world state

One pattern, three systems, authoring effort concentrated exactly where players form memories and tell stories.

03

The Power Model Locked

Four orthogonal axes. No single number decides a fight.

A single power stat makes builds meaningless — the bigger number wins and treasures are just addition. Splitting power across independent axes is what produces build diversity and gives each breakthrough something qualitative to unlock.

AxisTypeRole
RealmStepThe exponential gate. Crossing it is a discontinuity, not growth. Also gates which mechanics exist at all.
TierLinearGrindable progress inside a realm. Where routine cultivation and hunting pay out.
AffinityVectorThe build axis. Weighted elements determining skill and treasure synergy. Not a class pick.
InsightDiscreteComprehension slots filled by events, not grinding. Each grants a verb, not a stat. Can beat realm.

Character shape

character = {
  realm:     int,
  tier:      int,
  affinity:  { element: weight, ... },   # the build
  insights:  [ insight_id, ... ],        # the identity
  treasures: [ treasure_id, ... ],
  stability: float,                      # meridian integrity
  foundation: float                      # invisible until it isn't
}

Realm and tier alone never tell you how a fight resolves. That is the load-bearing property of the whole design — it is what lets Act Three's "power stops deciding things" turn land as a mechanic instead of an assertion.

04

Realms Locked

Each breakthrough unlocks a verb, not a multiplier.

Breakthroughs are threshold events with real failure risk — botch the tribulation and you lose progress, gain a flaw, or worse. What makes them feel like progression rather than an idle-game milestone is that each one changes what you can do, not how hard you hit.

RealmNameUnlocks
1Body TemperingPhysical technique only. No true magic. Affinity is latent and inert.
2Qi AwakenedAffinity manifests. Elemental skills and treasure resonance come online.
3World-TouchedDraw on ambient world energy, not just internal qi. Area effects, formations, arrays. built
4Concept-BoundFirst Insight slot opens. Comprehension begins to outperform raw tier.
5Soul-ManifestSpirit projection, split actions, remote effect. Treasure Wills become negotiable partners.
6+—Reality-adjacent effects. Numeric display begins abstracting into tiers.

Unlocks are boolean flags the systems check — can_use_world_energy, insight_slots — rather than stat thresholds. Content gates by realm without new code per realm.

The realm-6 abstraction is a tone device as much as a UI one: once power passes legibility, the game stops pretending the numbers mean anything, which is precisely the observation the genre never makes about itself.

05

Affinities Locked

Two layers, so "supreme" elements never compete with build identity.

Objectively superior affinities are only a problem when they sit on the same axis as build choice. The fix is to take Void, Space, and Time off that menu entirely. They are not elements — they are a separate layer that breaks rules rather than dealing damage.

Layer one — affinities (your build)

Horizontally balanced. This is where the synergy math lives.

ClassMembersNotes
CoreFire, Water, Wood, Metal, EarthWuxing generation and destruction cycles built in.
DerivedLightning, Ice, Storm…Unlock by holding both parent elements above a threshold. Immune to dilution once formed.
AspectBlood, Death, Poison, BeastThe morally costly paths. Better scaling, karma and social cost.

Layer two — fundamentals (rule-breakers)

Not affinities. They occupy Insight slots, are acquired mid-game, and are rare. Each counters a different system rather than a damage type — which is exactly why the setting calls them supreme, since they are the only things that touch the realm gap at all.

  • Space — counters engagement. Escape anything, reach anything, control distance and formations.
  • Time — counters action economy. Extra actions, rewinds. Strongest, rarest, costliest.
  • Void — counters the affinity system itself.

Because they live in the insight track, everyone can eventually reach this layer and it never competes with elemental identity. Build diversity stays entirely in layer one.

Void, specifically

Void is the zero vector — not a special case in the data model, but the absence of one. It ignores the relation matrix entirely and takes a flat ~0.6× with everything, immune to backlash.

MetricFire specialistVoid
Average on random loot0.64×0.60×
Floor0.00×0.60×
Dead items held2 in 5none
Overcap on unmatched rank+20.50× control1.10× control

Corrected in implementation

This section originally claimed Void wins late because breadth compounds — higher average yield across many slots. Measured, that is false: with only the five wuxing elements there are no unrelated pairs, so the 0.2 floor never fires and a specialist averages 0.64× on random loot, beating Void's flat 0.6×.

Void's real edge is its floor. A specialist holds a completely dead item 2 times in 5; Void never does, and never suffers backlash. That is what matters for uniques whose element you cannot choose, and for overcap reach — a specialist can only safely reach past their realm with matched gear, which is rare at high rank precisely because uniques are authored rather than farmed.

The arc still lands the way Act One needs it to. Early game, every NPC insists Void is the supreme protagonist affinity while the arithmetic quietly punishes you for believing them.

Where the deconstruction lands

Each fundamental taxes the relationship system. Time burns lifespan. Space means you are never here, so companion bonds decay faster. Void erodes identity, lowering relationship capacity outright — you become less legible as a person.

The three affinities that best solve the exponential realm gap are the three that hollow out the thing Act Three reveals actually mattered. The player who optimizes hardest for power arrives at the ending most alone, having done it entirely with numbers displayed on screen the whole time.

06

Synergy Math Locked

One formula, with the elemental cycles baked into a matrix.

Give the wuxing relations a matrix R — +1 same, +0.5 generating, -1 destroying, 0 unrelated — and genre-accurate behavior falls out of a single cosine-similarity expression for free.

Treasure effectiveness

match     = Σᵢⱼ (Aᵢ/|A|₁) × Rᵢⱼ × (Tⱼ/|T|₁)     # ∈ [-1, 1]
synergy   = clamp(0.2 + 1.3 × match, 0, 1.5)
effective = base_power
          × realm_scalar(realm, treasure.rank)
          × synergy

Corrected in implementation

This was originally specified as cosine similarity, which is wrong for this matrix. Because R has positive off-diagonal entries, cosine lets match exceed 1.0 — a fire/wood 50/50 build scored higher with a fire blade than a pure fire build did, since wood feeds fire. That deletes the dilution tradeoff entirely and makes fire+wood a free lunch.

L1 weighting fixes it. Match becomes the weighted average relation of your affinity mix to the treasure, so every dilution costs something — and diluting toward a generating element costs roughly 4× less than diluting toward a destroying one, which is a better decision space than cosine offered.

CharacterTreasureRelationResult
Pure FireFire bladeIdentity1.5×
WoodFire bladeGenerating~0.85×
UnrelatedAnyOrthogonal0.2×
WaterFire bladeDestroying0× + backlash

Two consequences worth protecting:

  • Loot is near-junk to the wrong build. That is a feature — it makes treasure hunting build-directed and creates a real player economy.
  • Dilution is a genuine tradeoff, for free. Adding a second element lowers normalized alignment with your first. "Do I accept this Lightning affinity?" becomes a real decision, and derived affinities are the reward for taking the risk.
07

Overcap Risk Locked

Reaching past your realm costs control, not power.

If high-rank gear simply deals less damage, it is a worse item and nobody is tempted. If it delivers full absurd damage and you cannot aim it, you get the actual genre beat: the kid holding a sword that really can split the mountain, and really might split him.

That reframes overcap from an efficiency loss into a variance trade — and variance is strategically correct when you are losing. Players will reach for overcap exactly when the realm gap is crushing them, which is the genre's signature move, and the math will agree with them.

The curve

gap     = treasure.rank - character.realm
power   = base × 5^gap        # full power, always
control = 0.45^gap            # ...but your grip collapses
GapPowerControlFeel
0×1100%Your weapon. Deterministic.
+1×545%Risky edge. Viable with support.
+2×2520%Desperate gambit.
+3×1259%Suicide with a lottery ticket attached.
+5——Hard cap. Cannot activate. You can carry it, and want to.

Expected value rises with gap, so overcap is never strictly wrong — but backlash severity scales with the treasure's power, so the downside rises just as fast. Use the formula for architecture; make it table-overridable per rank so the steps can be hand-tuned later.

What raises control

Control modifiers

effective_control =
    base_control
  × (0.5 + synergy)      # affinity match — "this is MY sword"
  + will_disposition     # sentient treasures choose
  + insight_bonus        # comprehension over realm
  × stability_factor     # current meridian integrity

The will term is the important one. For sentient treasures, overcap is not a dice roll against physics — it is a negotiation. A legendary blade will let a weak wielder swing it if it likes them, or wants something from them.

So there are two routes past the realm gap: gamble with variance, or build a relationship. A player who treats every treasure as a resource to be extracted only ever gets the first one. The same lesson as the companion system, arrived at through loot.

The cost ladder

  1. Stability — meridian integrity

    Drains on every overcap use, success included. Slow to recover, and low stability lowers control further. The real limiter: you can overcap, just not repeatedly. Death spirals are possible and dramatic.

  2. Injuries & flaws

    Persistent traits from bad backlash. Cracked Meridian: −20% qi recovery. They accumulate and need rare treasures or long recovery to clear — which sends you hunting, which tempts you to overcap.

  3. Foundation damage

    Permanent, and it does not touch your stats at all. It degrades breakthrough odds at future realms. Invisible in Act One by design.

Act Three payload

Tier three is the deconstruction, mechanized. Foundation damage moves nothing in Act One — you are punching above your weight and it is awesome. Then at the Realm 5 tribulation, a player who spent the early game gambling discovers their foundation is compromised and they cannot break through. Permanently capped, by choices the game rewarded forty hours earlier.

The elder frowns at your meridians. "You've been reaching past your grasp. Your foundation is flawed."

Every xianxia protagonist ignores the cautious elder, and every xianxia protagonist is right to. Here they are wrong. The character sheet has read Foundation: Flawed for thirty hours, and nobody read it, because the genre trained them not to. The deconstruction lands without a single cutscene.

08

Treasures Locked

One schema. Variety comes from combination, not bespoke code.

Treasure schema

{
  id, name, rank,
  category,                 # weapon / armor / accessory / core / material
  affinity_tags: { element: weight, ... },
  effects: [ { hook, magnitude, condition } ],
  growth: null | { xp, stages: [...] },
  will:   null | { disposition, demands: [...] }
}

Authoring tiers

Tier the authoring effort so only what deserves hand-writing gets hand-written.

RankGenerationScarcity
1–2Fully procedural from material inputsInfinite. Zero authoring.
3–4Named templates with variable rollsA Frostfang Blade. Several exist.
5+Hand-authored, world-uniqueExactly one exists. Tracked in world state.

That top tier is an architecture decision, not just a content one: uniques need a holder field and live in world state, not in a loot table. There are twelve rank-5 treasures in the world; each is somewhere, and if you do not have it, a named NPC does. It can be won at auction, negotiated for, or taken off their body.

The loyalty counterpoint

The growth field is the counterweight to overcap's "grab everything shiny" pull. A rank-1 blade carried since the tutorial can, with investment, outperform the rank-4 artifact looted last week. It is genre-authentic, emotionally satisfying, and thematically identical to the companion argument: the thing you stayed with outperforms the thing you grabbed.

09

Loot & Economy Locked

Monsters do not drop treasures. Monsters drop monster.

That single rule kills the randomness problem. A fire-affinity rank-2 wolf dropping fire-wolf materials is completely consistent; that same wolf dropping a sword never is. Treasures get made from monster.

MaterialsTreasures
SourceHarvested, farmed, table-drivenCrafted, bought, taken
FungibleYes — stackable, tradeableNo
VolumeHighLow
RandomnessQuality varies, existence does notAuthored or player-directed

Anatomy is the loot table

Derived from the monster's own stats rather than hand-written per creature:

  • Core — always present. Rank and affinity inherited from the monster. The universal currency.
  • Body parts — scale, claw, hide, bone, sinew, blood. Each carries the monster's affinity.
  • Rare organ — heart, marrow, eye. Low chance, high value. The only real drop-roll in the system.

How you kill it matters

The same corpse yields differently depending on kill method, harvesting skill, and time spent. Burn a beast and the hide is ruined; precise kills preserve organs. This produces a real tactical tension — your best element is often the one that destroys the drops — and makes combat decisions matter past winning. Harvesting properly also spends from the daily action pool.

The economy writes itself

A freebie from the R matrix: you cannot efficiently farm your own element. Fire beasts resist Fire, so a Fire cultivator hunting Fire materials fights at a structural disadvantage — while slaughtering Water beasts effortlessly and collecting Water materials they can barely use.

Everyone efficiently farms exactly what they cannot use. Trade becomes structurally necessary rather than a bolted-on shop, and "I need Frost Marrow, that is rank-3 ice beasts in the Northern Reach, four-day trip" is a quest the material system generates for free — enormously valuable for a solo developer who cannot hand-write hundreds of them.

Acquisition channels

ChannelCarriesCharacter
HarvestMaterialsVolume. Gated by combat skill, harvesting, and time.
ShopsMaterials, low-rank goodsReliable, overpriced, deliberately boring. The safe floor.
AuctionsRare treasuresScarce and contested — NPCs bid, you can lose. Also how you learn what exists.
Theft & bodiesUniquesWhere the best gear actually moves. And the moral channel.

The Will remembers

Xianxia protagonists murder rivals and pocket their gear constantly, framed as unremarkable. Give it teeth: a sentient artifact taken off a corpse carries a permanent will_disposition penalty toward you, which feeds straight into the overcap control formula in §07.

Murdered-for treasures grant less control when overcapping — the gear you took the shortcut for is precisely the gear that will not cooperate when you are desperate. Stolen power fails you at the exact moment the genre promises it will save you. No sermon required; it is just the numbers.

Net result: randomness survives only in rare-organ rolls, material quality, and auction competition. Everything else is authored or player-directed.

10

Crafting & Refining Locked

Composition, not recipes. The craft itself has to contain the decision.

Crafting systems usually fail for one reason: they are recipe lookup. Gather X, press button, receive Y. The only decision happened when you chose to gather X, so the craft is either trivial or, repeated, grind. Minigames do not fix this — they add interaction without adding decision, and a terminal forecloses them anyway.

So there are no recipes. The player picks a form — blade, robe, ring, talisman — and feeds materials into it. Everything else derives.

Craft resolution

affinity_tags = normalize(Σ mat.affinity × mat.quality × qty)
rank          = f(highest_rank_input, total_quality)
effects       = union of material hooks    # venom sac → on_hit poison
growth        = if any input is growth-bearing
will          = only from intact soul-bearing material

No recipe database to author, infinite variety from finite materials, and the player already reads the math because §06 taught it to them.

The decision: sharp or strong

Two axes fight, and this tension is the entire game of crafting.

PurityPotency
MethodFew materials, one elementMore and rarer materials, higher rank
GainsHigh synergy, up to 1.5×High base power
CostsLower base powerDiluted vector, synergy collapses
Wanted bySpecialistsVoid cultivators

That last row is a free gift from §05: Void ignores affinity match entirely, so Void cultivators are the natural buyers of powerful, muddied gear that every specialist considers ruined. The economy deepens without new systems.

The crafter shapes variance, not the mean

Crafting always produces something — losing materials outright is pure feel-bad. What varies is quality, rolled on a distribution, and the crafter shapes that distribution.

  • Low skill — wide spread, low ceiling. You get an item; you do not get a good one.
  • High skill — narrow spread, high ceiling.
  • Master, well-disposed — can exceed the normal ceiling. Inspired work.

Same materials, different hands, genuinely different outcome. That is what makes crafter relationships load-bearing rather than decorative.

Crafter NPCs

Split by the governing principle in §02. Village artisans generate — every settlement rolls two to four from a template with craft type, skill, affinity specialty, a personality trait, and a want. They are services, and fungible. Masters are written — eight to twelve exist in the world, each with history, a reason to refuse you, and something they want.

Masters are not vendors with a skill check. They refuse you initially, and they judge your materials' provenance: a master who reveres beasts will not work with something you tortured, and one with scruples will not touch a component stripped off a corpse. Combat method chains into harvest quality chains into crafting access.

The spine

Companions, treasure Wills, and crafters are the same mechanic on three surfaces: instrumentalize me and I resist. One system to build, three places it pays off.

If a master crafter is romanceable, the genre's version is "acquire blacksmith waifu, receive free legendary gear forever." Here she has expectations — time, presence, reciprocity — and treating her as a gear vending machine drives the same resentment stat companions use, which closes off inspired work. The best gear in the game is gated behind not doing the thing the genre trains you to do.

Anti-grind constraints

Three hard rules that kill the iron-ingot problem outright.

  1. No intermediate materials

    No ore → ingot → bar. Monster part goes straight into treasure. The only refinement step is purification — spending days to raise a material's quality, which is an action-economy trade rather than a chore.

  2. Crafting is rare, not routine

    Three or four significant items per realm. The daily action pool enforces it: crafting costs days, so every craft is an event.

  3. No bulk consumables

    Pills and talismans are bought from shops. Crafting is for treasures only, which removes the repetition vector entirely.

Refining — the second life

Refining improves what you already carry, and it is where growth becomes something you do rather than something that accrues:

  • Feed materials to a growth-capable treasure to advance a stage
  • Re-forge to shift an existing item's affinity vector — expensive, risky, can ruin it
  • Repair damage and clear flaws

This makes the loyalty counterpoint in §08 real. Investing in the tutorial blade means forge trips and materials you chose not to spend elsewhere — an actual commitment, which is what makes it land when it outperforms the looted artifact.

Self-crafting is the isolation build

The player can craft personally, starting unskilled and improving with time investment. Since time is the scarce resource, self-sufficiency costs cultivation days and realm progress.

It is a real build choice, and it is the theme on a third surface: the player who wants to need nobody can do exactly that, and pays in realm and in relationships. They reach the endgame competent, independent, and alone — having chosen it one forge visit at a time.

11

Monsters Locked

A bestiary that composes instead of one that gets written.

Monster composition

monster = Family × Affinity × Rank × Region

Family    → anatomy, combat role, stat shape
Affinity  → damage, resistance, material element
Rank      → scale
Region    → flavor and an optional modifier trait

Fire × Serpent × Rank 2 is fully determined and completely logical: fire serpent parts, serpent tactics. Eight families across nine affinities and six ranks yields hundreds of coherent monsters from one small table.

Critically, each family is the origin of a material property the crafting system needs — which is where growth and will actually come from.

FamilyCombat roleKey materialsFeeds

Sentient treasures now have a specific origin: you have to hunt spirits for soul remnants. An entire content pillar fell out of the taxonomy for free, and it means the players who want negotiating partners for overcap (§07) have somewhere specific to go.

Against sameness

  • Attachable traits — a small pool (Ancient, Starving, Twin-Cored, Corrupted) that changes behavior and loot together, not just numbers.
  • Family × affinity genuinely diverges. A Fire Avian (fast, burning, holds Far) and an Earth Construct (slow, unkillable, must be out-damaged) share no tactics at all despite coming from the same generator.

Killing well vs. looting well

The tension from §09 gets concrete: fire ruins hide, overkill shatters the core, precise low-damage kills preserve organs. Meanwhile every extra round spent being careful is more ticks the monster gets to act. Fighting well and looting well are opposed, and §12 makes the cost of carefulness legible in the same currency as the fight.

Where the harvest decision lives

Harvesting costs from the daily action pool, but the choice belongs at the kill, not on the day menu — filed away as a separate errand it severs the link between how you fought and what you get, and it invites players to bank corpses. Offer three options over the body, with what the fight already ruined named up front:

  • Strip the core and move on — free, core-slot parts only, but guaranteed
  • Take it apart properly — costs an action, full anatomy roll at full care
  • Leave it — free, nothing

This keeps the action-economy cost that makes harvesting compete with cultivating, while turning it into a live judgement: a rank-1 wolf is rarely worth a third of your day, and an Ancient Twin-Cored one always is.

12

Combat Locked

Tick-based, so the realm gap is felt as frequency rather than damage.

Turn-based is correct for the architecture, but strict alternating turns is the wrong flavor. Give every combatant a speed stat and every action a time cost; whoever has the lowest accumulated time acts next. Roguelikes have used this energy model for decades and it is barely harder to build.

The payoff is that the realm gap becomes action frequency.

The gap, felt

Realm +1 enemy  →  acts ~3× per your action
Realm +2 enemy  →  acts ~9× per your action

"This enemy hits for 40,000" is a number. Watching something take five actions before you take one is terror — and it is precisely what the genre means when it calls a realm gap unbridgeable. Not that they hit harder, but that you cannot react.

It also gives Time affinity something granular to manipulate. If turns are atomic, "extra action" is a blunt instrument; with variable time costs, Time can shave costs, act out of sequence, or steal ticks outright.

Structure

  • Range bands, not a grid — Close / Mid / Far. Moving costs time; techniques have band requirements. Cheap in a terminal, gives Space affinity a surface to break, and makes Avians play unlike Constructs.
  • A tight action menu — Strike · Technique · Treasure · Move · Steady · Flee. Steady recovers stability, which matters because of §07.

Beating something above your realm

Act Three requires that raw power stops deciding fights, so the alternatives have to be real mechanics present from the start.

RouteMechanic
OvercapThe variance gamble (§07)
InsightVerbs that bypass rules — Space escapes, Time steals ticks
FormationsRealm 3 unlock — slow, trap, or split the enemy's ticks
AffinityThe R matrix; the correct counter-element does real work
PreparationAmbush, poisons, terrain — sourced from harvesting knowledge
CompanionsMore bodies means more actions

Action economy

Alone vs Realm+1:          1 action per its 3
With 3 loyal companions:   4 actions per its 3

The thesis, as combat math

In a tick system allies are literally action economy. The power fantasy says get stronger. The math says do not be alone.

And it is not a special case bolted on for the theme — it is simply how the system works. Companions who resent you fight sloppily, arrive late, or do not come at all, so a player who collected them as trophies has bodies but not actions. The harem critique resolves in the initiative order.

13

Companions & Time Needs detail

The scarcity that makes the harem trope into a real tradeoff.

The time economy is the load-bearing piece. A limited pool of daily actions splits across cultivating, exploring, harvesting, and socializing. Power-fantasy protagonists have infinite time for everything; making time genuinely scarce is what turns companions from free rewards into opportunity cost.

Each companion carries a relationship stat and an autonomy/resentment stat. Under-invested companions do not sit idle waiting to be collected — they act. They leave, they resent, they undermine, and they can take techniques and secrets with them.

This is the same argument the treasure Will system makes, which is the point: things you try to purely instrumentalize resist you. Both systems must exist in the MVP, even minimally, or the Act Three turn has nothing to land on.

§12 is what gives this section teeth. Companions are action economy, so relationship investment is not a side activity competing with power — past a certain realm gap it is the power. What still needs specifying: the resentment curve, what companions do autonomously when neglected, and the roster itself.

MVP Scope Built

Small enough to finish, complete enough to prove every loop end to end.

Status

Built and playable. Python 3.14, no dependencies, all content data-driven. Three test suites cover the synergy math, every system headless, and a scripted 40-day integration run.

Two design claims did not survive implementation and are corrected in §05 and §06 — the synergy formula's normalization, and what Void's advantage actually is.

  • Realm 1, plus the breakthrough into Realm 2 — the "physical only → affinity awakens" qualitative jump
  • 4–5 core affinities with the full R matrix and synergy formula
  • 3 monster families × 3 affinities, with anatomy-derived material tables
  • 5–8 treasures exercising synergy, overcap, growth, and at least one will
  • Tick-based combat with range bands and the realm-gap frequency curve
  • Daily action allocation loop covering cultivate / hunt / harvest / craft / socialize
  • Composition crafting with one procedural village artisan
  • One fully realized companion with a personal arc and working resentment
  • Stability and foundation tracked and displayed from day one, even before they bite

Everything above lives in data files. Phase 2 ("add a realm", "add five monsters", "add a companion") must be authoring work, not engineering work — that constraint is what makes the full-game ambition survivable solo.
